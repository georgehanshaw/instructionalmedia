Lesson outline

Why developmental theories matter

Developmental theories are essential when looking at development through the lifespan.

They help you answer important questions, such as:

How does a person grow and develop physically, cognitively, socially, emotionally, spiritually, and morally?

In this short lesson you will explore each of these six areas of development and then practice recognizing them in everyday examples.

Six areas of development

Each area is a different lens for looking at the same person. Select an area to read about it, or use Expand all to open every card.

The question: How does a person grow and develop physically?

Changes in the body and brain over time: growth, motor skills such as crawling and walking, health, puberty, and the physical changes of aging.

The question: How does a person grow and develop cognitively?

Changes in thinking: attention, memory, language, problem solving, and reasoning, from a baby exploring objects to an adult weighing abstract ideas.

The question: How does a person grow and develop socially?

Changes in how a person relates to others: forming friendships, cooperating, belonging to a family or community, and taking on social roles.

The question: How does a person grow and develop emotionally?

Changes in how a person understands, expresses, and manages feelings, including bonds of attachment and coping with stress.

The question: How does a person grow and develop spiritually?

Changes in a person's search for meaning, purpose, and connection to something larger than themselves, including faith and worldview.

The question: How does a person grow and develop morally?

Changes in how a person understands right and wrong, develops values and conscience, and decides how to act toward others.

The six areas are connected. A change in one area, such as starting school, often involves several others at once.

Summary

Developmental theories let you look at the whole person across the lifespan.

  • Physical: the body and brain
  • Cognitive: thinking and learning
  • Social: relationships with others
  • Emotional: feelings and how we manage them
  • Spiritual: meaning, purpose, and faith
  • Moral: right and wrong, values and conscience
