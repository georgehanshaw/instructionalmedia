Lesson outline

What is culture?

Culture has been described as the values, beliefs, and behaviors that are passed down from generation to generation.

These areas have an effect on the way people develop during their lifespan. In this module, we will examine some of the differences and similarities in various cultures.

Values
What a group considers important.
Beliefs
What a group holds to be true.
Behaviors
How members of a group typically act.

Independence and social inhibition

There are vast cultural differences that affect how individuals are influenced in childhood, but there are also similarities. One difference is how a culture views independence and shyness. Select a tab to compare.

Western cultures

Good traits: Children are raised to be independent and to show assertive behavior.

Unfavorable traits: Socially-inhibited and reticent behavior is looked at as being fearful, shy, and socially unacceptable.

Eastern cultures

Good traits: Inhibited behavior and social wariness are considered good traits. Children are seen as well-mannered and obedient, which shows growth in social maturity.

Cooperation and competition

Another example of cultural difference is in the area of cooperation and competition. Select a tab to explore three comparisons.

Interdependent and Western cultures

In interdependent cultures, cooperation is emphasized more than competition.

In Western cultures, the opposite tends to be true: competition is emphasized more than cooperation.

East Asia

Children in East Asian nations tend to be more cooperative with family and friends.

In education, however, they are more competitive.

Mexican Americans

There can also be generational differences. Third-generation Mexican Americans tend to have a much stronger competitive desire than the second generation.

Similarities: prosocial behavior

Similarities also exist between cultures. Prosocial behavior is an example of these similarities.

Caring for others is a behavior that is homogeneous (the same) across cultures.

Being polite is a behavior that is homogeneous across cultures.

Being helpful is a behavior that is homogeneous across cultures.

Among Asian cultures these behaviors seem to be more prevalent. Asian parents encourage their preschool children to help and share with other children as a way of promoting social conventions.

Putting it together

The development of a child's behavior is influenced by their culture.

  • While there are differences between cultures, there are also some universal norms.
  • These behaviors can also differ in children of immigrants, who are affected by the introduction of new cultural influences.
