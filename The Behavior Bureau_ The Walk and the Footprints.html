The Walk and the Footprints

Every client file lands on your desk with two kinds of clues mixed together. One is what the person did. The other is what was left behind. Your job is to tell them apart.

Open the case file

Know your two kinds of evidence

A behavioral assessment starts with a clear record of what a person does. Reports often skip that and describe what happened afterward, like a grade, a weight, or a messy room. Those are real and useful, but they are a different kind of clue.

Behavior

What a person does. You can watch it happen, and you can count it or time it.

Behavior outcome

What a behavior produces or changes. It is the result, product, or state left behind.

Two quick tests

The camera test

Could a video camera record it happening? If yes, it is a behavior. If you would only see it by looking at what remains afterward, it is an outcome.

The dead man’s test

Could a dead person do it? If yes, it is not a behavior. A dead person can “lose six pounds” or “have a clean room.” They cannot walk or sort laundry.

The behavior. Someone walked across the sand. You can watch it, count the steps, and time it.

    What to carry back to your own files

    • Describe the behavior first: what the person did, in words a camera could confirm.
    • Treat outcomes as pointers. A grade, a weight, or a scrap count tells you where to look, not what to change.
    • When a report gives only an outcome, ask what the person was doing when it was produced.
