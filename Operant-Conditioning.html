What is operant conditioning?

Operant conditioning is learning that occurs when an association is formed between a behavior and the consequences of that behavior.

The key components of operant conditioning are positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, positive punishment, and negative punishment. You will explore each one in this lesson.

Everyday examples

Operant conditioning is at work in all of these situations. Select a card to see the behavior and its consequence.

Behavior: The dog fetches the ball.

Consequence: It has been praised for doing so in the past.

Behavior: The bully threatens his classmates.

Consequence: Doing so allows him to get his way.

Behavior: The child gets good grades.

Consequence: Her parents threaten to punish her if she doesn't.

Thorndike and the law of effect

Operant conditioning was first studied by psychologist Edward L. Thorndike. Step through his experiment one moment at a time.

Thorndike's puzzle box

  1. Thorndike placed cats in a puzzle box from which they had to try to escape.
  2. The cats scratched and bit the box until they accidentally pressed a lever that opened the door.
  3. The open door revealed both the exit and a reward of fish.
  4. Over the next several attempts in the puzzle box, the cats learned to repeat the effective response in order to escape quickly.

The law of effect

From observing the changes in the cats' behavior, Thorndike developed his law of effect.

  • A successful response with a pleasant outcome is more likely to be repeated.
  • Conversely, an unsuccessful response with an unpleasant outcome will occur less frequently.

Skinner and the key terms

Behavioral psychologist B. F. Skinner expanded on Thorndike's ideas through experimentation on rats. Much like Thorndike's cats in the puzzle box, Skinner created a puzzle box for rats with levers to release food or water.

He studied how the rats' behavior changed through reinforcement and punishment, and he developed terms to describe the processes of operant learning.

Reinforcer
Any event that increases the likelihood of a behavior.
Punisher
Any event that decreases the likelihood of a behavior.
Positive and negative
Skinner used these terms to refer to whether a reinforcement was added or subtracted.

The four types

Reinforcement increases the likelihood of a behavior, while punishment decreases it. The words positive and negative describe whether something is added or removed.

Positive reinforcement

Increases the likelihood of a response by adding something pleasant after the response.

Consider these examples of positive reinforcement:

  • A little girl receives an allowance for doing her chores.
  • A mother buys her son a toy for doing the dishes.
  • A father gives his daughter verbal praise for completing her homework.

Negative reinforcement

Increases the likelihood of a response by removing something unpleasant after the response.

Consider these examples of negative reinforcement:

  • A man takes an aspirin to take away the pain from his headache.
  • A woman enters a code that turns off a loud alarm.
  • A young boy can leave the dinner table after he eats his vegetables.

Positive punishment

Decreases the likelihood of a response by adding something unpleasant after the response.

Consider these examples of positive punishment:

  • A child burps loudly in class and is reprimanded by the teacher in front of the entire class.
  • A child touches a hot stove and feels pain.
  • A man drinks spoiled milk and gets a bad taste in his mouth.

Negative punishment

Decreases the likelihood of a response by removing something pleasant after the response.

Consider these examples of negative punishment:

  • A student copies the answers from another student's test, so his teacher removes his test score from his total grade.
  • A child pulls a girl's hair in class, so his teacher does not reward him with good behavior points that could have earned him a prize.
  • An older brother picks on his younger brother, so his parents take away his favorite video game.

Review and check

In review, reinforcement works by increasing the likelihood of a behavior, while punishment works by decreasing the likelihood of a behavior.

  • Positive reinforcement or punishment is when something is added in order to change the behavior.
  • Negative reinforcement or punishment is when something is removed in order to change the behavior.

As the examples show, operant conditioning can be used to modify behavior using positive and negative consequences.

It is important to note that research suggests that positive consequences produce better results than negative consequences. It is suggested that positive interventions be attempted before using negative ones.
