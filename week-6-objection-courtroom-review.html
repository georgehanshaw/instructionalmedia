Objection! Review Like a Courtroom

Opposing counsel will read every line of an officer’s report. Decide which lines to challenge and why.

How it works

You are reviewing excerpts from a fictional officer’s report, line by line. For each line, choose Objection! if a courtroom could challenge it, or Let it stand if it is solid documentation. If you object, choose the ground. Correct objection with the right ground: 2 points. Correct objection with a different valid ground: 1 point. Correctly letting a solid line stand: 2 points.

Grounds for objection (reference)
GroundWhat it means
Conclusion without factsA label or judgment with no observable facts to back it up.
Passive voiceHides who did the action.
No legal justificationAn action is reported, but not the legal basis for it.
Jargon or codesTerms a judge, juror, or reader outside the agency may not understand.
Plans instead of actionsDocuments what the officer intends to do rather than what happened.
Missing legal elementsA charge is named without the facts that satisfy each element.
Opinion or subjective languagePersonal feelings or characterizations instead of observations.
